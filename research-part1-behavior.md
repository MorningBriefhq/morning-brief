# Morning Brief — Research Part 1: What Self-Directed Investors Actually Read in the Morning

Research date: October 12, 2026. Methods: web search (news + general verticals) and page-text fetches. Each finding is labeled **sourced** (named study with methodology) or **inference** (plausible reading of data, not directly measured).

---

## 1. Investor morning routines: what gets checked first, and when

**Finding 1.1 — Social media has overtaken traditional sources as Gen Z's #1 financial-news source, and is rising fast across cohorts.**
- **Stat:** Social media is Gen Z's most commonly cited source for financial news, up from 45% in 2024 to 60% in 2026 — nearly 3× the rate who cite a financial advisor (21%).
- **Source:** Betterment 2026 Retail Investor Survey (released Aug 2026; 1,000 retail investors across Gen Z/Millennials/Gen X/Boomers, surveyed April 2026). PR Newswire.
- **Label:** Sourced.
- **So what:** The 40s tech-native cohort (older Millennials / younger Gen X) sits between Gen Z's social-native habit and Boomers' advisor/TV habit — a personalized morning brief competes as much against X/Reddit/YouTube as against Bloomberg.

**Finding 1.2 — FINRA confirms social + message boards as decision inputs for the majority of young investors.**
- **Stat:** 60% of investors aged 18–34 use social media or message boards to help make investment decisions; 61% have acted on a tip from a social media personality.
- **Source:** FINRA Foundation (via ImpactWealth, 2025 coverage; underlying survey of young investors).
- **Label:** Sourced (numbers via secondary coverage; methodology details not independently verified).
- **So what:** Portfolio-relevant news must be framed as "what this means for your money," because the competing content these readers trust is personality-driven and tip-flavored.

**Finding 1.3 — Newer investors' top information sources are video (36%), social (35%), investing platforms (31%), and personal finance websites (31%) — traditional news media is at 22%.**
- **Source:** Commonwealth "Understanding Today's Retail Investor" report (buildcommonwealth.org, PDF, Oct 2025). Respondents = investors <5 yrs experience; multiple-select.
- **Label:** Sourced.
- **So what:** Brokerage/platform surfaces are themselves a top-4 source. A digest that reads like an in-app briefing (holdings-first, plain-English "why it moved") matches the revealed mental model.

**Finding 1.4 — Professional routine pattern: indices/futures first, headlines second, own holdings last (or first).**
- Practitioner write-ups converge on the same sequence: check major indices + equity futures + 10-yr/2-yr yields and overnight markets → scan headlines → then look at own positions. (TradersUnion citing Sonali Basak; medium.com Finviz routine; bullsonwallstreet.com.)
- **Label:** Inference (anecdotal practitioner consensus, not survey data). No large-sample study on "what investors check first in the morning" was found — this is a data gap.
- **So what:** Mirror that sequence in the brief: market snapshot first (futures, yields, overnight), then "what moved and why," then portfolio-specific news. Don't lead with portfolio P&L — pros check context before positions.

**Finding 1.5 — Newsletters are a minority pathway to news, but age-neutral.**
- **Stat:** Only 3 in 10 U.S. adults get news from email newsletters at least "sometimes" (6% "often," 24% "sometimes," 26% "rarely," 43% "never"). Unlike most digital formats, uptake is roughly equal across age groups.
- **Source:** Pew Research Center, "Email Newsletters as a Source of News" (report Feb 19, 2026; survey of U.S. adults Aug 18–24, 2025).
- **Label:** Sourced.
- **So what:** The addressable base is real but newsletter-first investors are self-selected. Age-neutrality is good news: a brief designed for 40s investors can pull from adjacent cohorts without format friction.

## 2. Newsletter engagement benchmarks

**Finding 2.1 — Platform-wide newsletter benchmarks (2025).**
- **Stats (beehiiv, "State of Newsletters 2026," 28B emails analyzed, 2025 data):** 41.24% open rate (up from 37.67% in 2024), 3.23% CTR (down from 4.74% in 2024), 98.90% delivery. Best send time: early morning; best days Tue/Wed.
- **Label:** Sourced.
- **So what:** Open-rate floor for a decent newsletter is ~40% in 2025/26; CTR is compressing industry-wide (opens rising, clicks falling) — readers increasingly want the email to BE the content, not a teaser. Write the brief so nothing important requires a click.

**Finding 2.2 — Finance-specific benchmarks run lower than platform averages.**
- **Stats:** GetResponse 2024: financial services 34.70% open rate, 15.40% CTOR; Mailchimp: business & finance 31.35% open rate, 2.78% CTR. Realistic advisor-newsletter targets: 28–35% open, 2.5–3.5% CTR, 9–13% CTOR.
- **Source:** newsletterasaservice.com financial-advisor benchmarks (2026 compilation of GetResponse/Mailchimp/HubSpot data).
- **Label:** Sourced.
- **So what:** Finance newsletters cluster ~30–35% open. Anything consistently above 40% is genuinely elite in this category.

**Finding 2.3 — Best-in-class investor newsletters hit ~50%+ opens with tight hygiene.**
- Finimize's daily newsletter: 850k subscribers, **52% open rate**, explicitly "designed to be read over your morning coffee or commute"; their deep dives run 3–8 minutes. (finimize.com for Business, 2026; self-reported.)
- Morning Brew's daily: ~40% unique open rate (1.1M unique openers / 2.7M subs); they aggressively prune anyone who hasn't opened in 60 days — "we don't care about the vanity metric of total subscribers… we really care about total opens" (Digiday via Talking Biz News). (2021–22 era data; directionally still cited.)
- **Label:** Sourced (company-reported; pruning methodology matters for comparability).
- **So what:** The 50%+ club shares three traits: daily cadence, morning delivery, and ruthless list hygiene. Plan for engagement-based sunset pruning from day one.

**Finding 2.4 — Even among people who get news from newsletters, most don't read most of them.**
- **Stat:** Among U.S. adults who get news from email newsletters, **62% say they don't end up reading most of the newsletters they receive**; only 38% read (in part or in full) the bulk of what they get.
- **Source:** Pew Research Center, same Aug 2025 survey.
- **Label:** Sourced.
- **So what:** Inbox slot is cheap; attention slot is scarce. Subject line + first 100 words decide everything. Design for the 62% who will skim — the brief must deliver its value in skimmable form, not buried at the bottom.

**Finding 2.5 — MPP-era caveat: open rates are inflated.**
- The benchmarks page explicitly notes CTOR is the better content-quality signal "once you account for Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflation." Apple MPP inflates open rates (machine opens) across all benchmarks since 2021.
- **Label:** Sourced (widely established industry fact).
- **So what:** Track read-time and CTOR, not opens, as the quality metric. Consider "scrolled past 50%" as the primary engagement KPI.

## 3. The "5-minute read" claim: optimal length data

**Finding 3.1 — 51 seconds: the canonical average time-on-newsletter after opening.**
- **Stat:** Nielsen Norman Group eye-tracking study (2006; 117 newsletters, 42 participants): average time allocated to a newsletter after opening was **51 seconds**. Participants fully read only **19%** of newsletters; the dominant behavior was scanning. 35% of the time they only skimmed a small part or just glanced.
- **Label:** Sourced (old — 2006 — but still the most-cited study on the topic; behavior has if anything shifted further toward scanning since mobile).
- **Label note:** No large recent eye-tracking study of financial newsletters specifically was found — gap.
- **So what:** "5-minute read" is aspirational; realistically you own under a minute of attention unless you earn more. Budget: ~150–200 words of core content before the average reader checks out (≈51 sec at 200 wpm). Finimize's 3–8 minute deep-dive range (Finding 2.3) implies total emails run ~600–1,600 words, but the core value must land in the first 200.

**Finding 3.2 — IBM's email read-rate standards: 25% glance (<2 sec), 22% skim (2–8 sec), 53% read (8+ sec).**
- **Source:** RJI Online citing IBM engagement metrics.
- **Label:** Sourced.
- **So what:** Set the internal bar as "% of openers reaching 8+ seconds," not opens. A brief where >50% of openers hit 8+ seconds is performing at/above email norms.

**Finding 3.3 — News readers read briefs 3× more than articles; text beats graphics.**
- **Stat:** Stanford/Poynter Eyetrack study: participants read **3× as many briefs as full articles**; briefs viewed 82% of the time vs articles 92%, but readers preferred text over graphics/photos (67 participants, online news readers).
- **Label:** Sourced (2000-era study; directional finding has replicated since).
- **So what:** The brief format itself is the right container — scannable briefs over long-form. Keep body copy dominant; don't bury signal in charts/graphics.

**Finding 3.4 — Readability beats raw length; every extra structural element has a read-rate cost.**
- Internal-comms benchmark guidance: 800–1,200 words (5–7 min) is the ceiling for email newsletters; "if your newsletter takes 20 minutes to read, it won't get read" (Flipbooker best practices).
- **Label:** Inference (practitioner guidance, not controlled study). No published study was found pinpointing "completion collapses at X words."
- **So what:** Working rule: aim ≤800 words core body (~4 min at 200 wpm) with the essential payload in the first 200 words. Test empirically with scroll/read-time data rather than trusting a magic number — the collapse point hasn't been rigorously established for financial newsletters.

## 4. Morning vs evening consumption

**Finding 4.1 — Morning sends win opens; evenings win engagement — but for newsletters, morning is the industry default.**
- **Stats:** 9–11 AM local earns the highest open rates (active inbox scanning); 12–3 PM earns higher CTR; 7–9 PM shows strongest overall engagement in aggregate 2025 studies (massmailer.io synthesis). Mailchimp/Litmus/MailerLite data: US opens peak around **10 AM**, Tue–Thu.
- beehiiv's 28B-email dataset: **early morning is the strongest send time**, mid-afternoon secondary; avoid overnight.
- **Label:** Sourced (platform aggregate data; B2B/B2C blended).
- **So what:** For a pre-market brief, 7 AM ET is earlier than the 9–10 AM "open peak" but catches the pre-commute/pre-market window — a defensible trade: opens may be slightly lower than a 10 AM send, but pre-market utility (the product's whole point) only exists before 9:30 AM. Notably, massmailer.io warns morning readers skim — reinforcing the skimmable-design requirement.

**Finding 4.2 — The evening-engagement finding is a trap for this product.**
- 8 PM sends show ~14pp higher open rates than 2 PM sends with peak CTR (billions-of-emails analyses). But this measures generic email marketing; the mechanism (relaxed mindset, low inbox competition) doesn't create pre-market value.
- **Label:** Inference from sourced data.
- **So what:** Don't chase the evening slot. The pre-market window IS the product: decisions made before the bell are the only decisions a morning brief can influence. A post-close companion edition could be a future expansion, not a replacement.

**Finding 4.3 — Data gap: no direct survey found on pre-market vs post-close brief preference.**
- Practitioner opinion (medium.com/qastro, July 2026) argues pre-market briefs help traders understand overnight developments and pre-market sentiment before the bell. FinancialContent (June 2026) notes professionals increasingly convert newsletters into morning-commute audio briefings.
- **Label:** Honest gap — no A/B or survey data comparing morning vs evening financial-newsletter preference was found in this pass.
- **So what:** Validate with subscriber testing later (ask at signup: "morning pre-market / evening recap / both"); default to morning.

## 5. Push vs pull: alerts vs single digest

**Finding 5.1 — Notification fatigue is real and large: roughly half of users opt out.**
- **Stats:** Urban Airship: **64%** of users opt out of push; Ofcom: average push opt-out rate ~**50%**; Localytics: **52%** turn off push due to excessive messaging; 70% disable within 2–3 days of install; Leanplum: 70% unsubscribe due to frequency/irrelevance.
- 2024–26 benchmark (wisernotify): median Android opt-in **59.5%**, iOS **49.4%** — Android opt-in fell from 71.3% (2023) after Android 13's mandatory permission prompt.
- **Label:** Sourced.
- **So what:** A push-everything model will get silenced by half the audience within days. Pushes must be rare, triggered, and unambiguously worth an interruption.

**Finding 5.2 — Triggered, personalized pushes outperform generic broadcasts by ~3×.**
- **Stats:** Triggered push open rate **14.4%** vs generic **4.19%** (2025). Delivery rate: broadcast 80.73% vs behavior-triggered 93.07%.
- **Label:** Sourced (wisernotify compilation).
- **So what:** The only pushes that survive fatigue are behavioral triggers: your holding moved ±X%, earnings tomorrow, analyst change on your ticker. Never push "market is up today" — that's what the digest is for.

**Finding 5.3 — Push notifications demonstrably change investor behavior — which is a double-edged sword.**
- **Stat:** Robinhood's 5%-daily-move portfolio push notifications increased users' trading activity by **over 25%** (Moss, 2022 event study, master's thesis citing).
- **Label:** Sourced (academic study).
- **So what:** Pushes drive action — including impulsive action. For a decision-quality product, the bar for a push should be "would a good fiduciary interrupt you with this?" Position: the daily digest is the core product; push is an opt-in, user-configured exception layer (e.g., earnings day, ±5% moves on owned tickers, Fed days), defaulting to quiet.

**Finding 5.4 — What investors already pull: brokerage/news surfaces inside apps.**
- Robinhood's "Snacks" newsletter is auto-subscribed to every new user (daily, in-app); Finimize's whole model is "morning coffee or commute" multi-channel pull. Retail brokerage platforms (Robinhood, Webull, Futu, eToro: 41M+ funded accounts combined) all layer research/AI tools next to the trade button.
- **Label:** Inference from sourced product facts.
- **So what:** Investors pull news inside their brokerage app; the email digest competes on depth + personalization (their tickers, their sector weights), not on speed. Don't try to out-alert the brokerage app — out-explain it.

## Top 5 Most Actionable Findings (ranked)

1. **The 62% skim rule (Pew, 2026):** Even newsletter subscribers don't read most of what they get. → Design Morning Brief as skim-complete: full value in subject line + first 200 words; deeper sections are bonus, never the payload.
2. **Click-through is dying, opens are inflated (beehiiv 28B emails; MPP):** CTR fell 4.74%→3.23% while opens rose — readers want the email to BE the content. → Write self-contained briefs; optimize for read-time/CTOR, not clicks.
3. **Morning is the slot, but morning readers skim (platform data):** Early-morning Tue–Wed sends win for newsletters; 7 AM ET pre-market is earlier than the 9–10 AM open peak but is the only slot where the brief can influence pre-bell decisions. → Ship at 7 AM ET; accept slightly-lower opens vs 10 AM in exchange for decision utility.
4. **Triggered beats broadcast 3× on push (wisernotify):** ~50% opt-out rates kill generic push programs within days. → Push is opt-in, rare, behavioral (earnings tomorrow, ±5% on owned tickers, Fed days); the digest carries everything else.
5. **51-second attention budget (Nielsen Norman eye-tracking):** Average post-open attention is under a minute; full reads are the exception (19%). → Hard ceiling ~800 words, core value in first ~200; treat "every sentence must earn its place" as literally measurable via scroll/read-time analytics.
