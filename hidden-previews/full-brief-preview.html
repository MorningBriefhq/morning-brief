IMF warns AI crash threatens global financial stability
The International Monetary Fund warned that a sharp correction in AI shares could ripple across global markets, putting financial stability at risk. In its twice-yearly Global Financial Stability Report, the IMF said AI company valuations are "elevated" and their debt loads are rising. The fund noted that global exposure to U.S. AI assets is at historic highs, meaning a U.S.-led selloff would hit Asia hardest, with the UK and euro area somewhat insulated. This is the IMF's bluntest warning yet on AI concentration risk — it gives institutional investors cover to de-risk tech positions. The timing, alongside Broadcom's comments, amplifies the signal.
IMF: Global economy "could soon test the limits of resilience"
The IMF's World Economic Outlook warned that emergency oil stockpiles are now depleted after six months of releases to offset Middle East supply disruptions, leaving governments with little buffer against future price spikes. The fund said the rerouting of oil away from the Strait of Hormuz had contained damage so far, but high debt levels mean most governments can't afford another round of consumer subsidies. "Without shoring up supply capacity and replenishing policy buffers," the IMF warned, the next shock hits harder. Kuwait's announcement that it's ready to boost output from 2M bpd provides some comfort, but the margin for error is thin.
Sandisk: First crack in the memory supercycle?
Seeking Alpha flagged Sandisk as showing the first signs of weakness in the memory chip supercycle. This matters because memory has been one of the strongest AI-adjacent trades — if demand is rolling over, it confirms the broader chip anxiety. Combined with today's Asian memory selloff (SK Hynix -10%), the memory complex is under pressure from both fundamentals and sentiment. Nobel economics prize goes to Hall and Woodford for stabilization models
Robert Hall (Stanford) and Michael Woodford (Columbia) won the 2026 Nobel economics prize for developing the microeconomic foundations of macroeconomic stabilization — the models that help central bankers avoid recessions and runaway inflation. The Royal Swedish Academy cited their "paradigm shift in stabilisation policy." Their New Keynesian frameworks are the intellectual backbone of modern central banking, including the Fed's current approach. No direct market impact, but a reminder of whose models are guiding rate decisions right now.
IMF lifts UK growth forecast, urges Healey to "stay the course"
The IMF raised its UK growth forecast and praised the government's deficit-reduction plan, with chief economist Silvana Tenreyro saying Britain "stands out" among G7 nations for having a credible fiscal consolidation path. The endorsement comes two weeks before Chancellor Healey's first budget, where he's under pressure to announce energy subsidies. The IMF's message: don't blow up the deficit for short-term relief. For UK-exposed investors, this is a green light on fiscal credibility — gilt markets should take it well. The IFS separately warned the budget would be a "work of fiction" without clear funding plans, showing the domestic debate is sharper.
